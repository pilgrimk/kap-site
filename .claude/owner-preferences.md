# Standing Owner Preferences

The KAP owner's recurring design preferences, applied to every customer build. They are
**weighted preferences, not requirements**: they tilt a close decision and they shape which
candidates get considered, but they never decide a site against its own business fit or its
rendered evidence.

**Precedence** (`CLAUDE.md` §1): these rank below direct user instruction, the active brief and
`CLAUDE.md`, and above skills and skill defaults.

- **A brief that states its own design direction overrides them** for that project. That covers an
  archetype, a hero treatment, an imagery stance, a mood or a reference site.
- **No preference here relaxes `CLAUDE.md` §2.** Integrity, honest framing and production clearance
  always win.
- **No preference here relaxes §7.** Correctness, accessibility, the hero impact gate and the
  full-page impact gate always win.

---

## The preferences

1. **Premium, visually distinctive websites.** Each site should feel art-directed for its
   business, not assembled from a template. Competent and ordinary is the outcome to avoid.
2. **Full-bleed cinematic photographic heroes, when suitable.** The owner's preferred hero is the
   following, all designed together as one composition:
   - a photograph covering the whole hero, composed for the type that sits on it;
   - a deliberate subject position and negative space;
   - a large headline and supporting text overlaid on the image;
   - a prominent conversion CTA.

   "When suitable" is a real test, defined in `design-notes.md` (*Full-bleed photographic hero
   with a directional gradient*, suitability test).
3. **Dark directional gradients that support overlaid text.** Typically stronger on the side the
   copy occupies (usually the left), shaped so the subject stays visible rather than suppressed
   into texture.
4. **Strong typography and prominent conversion CTAs.** Confident display type and an obvious,
   high-contrast primary action.
5. **Business fit and customer response drive the decision.** The question is what makes this
   business's customers trust it and act, not what looks most impressive.
6. **Avoid generic split-column heroes**, meaning text on one side and a contained image on the
   other. Use one only when it genuinely outperforms the alternatives for this business, judged
   from the render.
7. **Preserve visual variety across sites.** The owner's portfolio should not converge on one
   look. A full-bleed hero is a preference, not a house template. Palette, type, image subject,
   scrim shape and composition still have to be decided fresh for each business.
8. **Visually rich pages beyond the hero.** Favor visually rich, engaging websites with
   purposeful photography and image-led sections beyond the hero where they strengthen the
   story. Avoid unnecessary decoration, generic imagery, and repetitive layouts. Visual richness
   must serve the business and its conversion goals.

## How they are applied

- **In the hero concept stage** (`site-build-workflow.md`, Phase 3): preference 2 means a
  full-bleed photographic concept is always one of the three candidates **when suitable**.
  Preferences 1, 5 and 6 mean the other candidates must be credible alternatives, never straw
  men.
- **In selection:** the preferences carry **meaningful weight**. Where candidates are close on the
  evidence, the preferred direction wins. Where another candidate is clearly stronger, or the
  preferred one fails an integrity, accessibility or mobile requirement, the stronger valid
  candidate wins and the record says why.
- **Never as critic input.** The fresh-context critic is not told these preferences. The critic
  supplies unanchored evidence; the build applies the weight afterwards and records that it did.
- **Variety (preference 7)** is checked against earlier customer builds as part of the concept
  stage and the whole-page visual strategy, so a preference does not silently become a repeated
  look.
- **In the whole-page visual strategy** (`site-build-workflow.md`, Phase 3): preference 8 weights
  each section's treatment toward purposeful photography where it strengthens that section. It
  is never a quota. A section the brief, honest framing, accessibility, trust or conversion is
  better served by typography stays typographic, with its reason recorded.

## Changing them

The owner edits this file. A change here applies to future builds only. Completed customer
repositories keep the snapshot of `.claude/` they were built with (`CLAUDE.md` §4.2) and are not
updated.
